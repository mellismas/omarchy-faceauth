# Security contract

What the daemon, the PAM module and the Omarchy pieces promise, one claim per bullet, each with the code that keeps it. Code pointers name a file under `faceauth-daemon/src/` and a function or type in it, unless another path is given. The [README](../README.md) carries the short form.

- **The lock screen looks; it does not prompt.** Its `omarchy-lock-face` stack is the face module closed by `pam_deny`. Its probe answers only yes or no to "likely you" (a best score of 0.5 or more on the probe's unflashed frame, under the 0.70 accept threshold on purpose), gives at most one fresh answer a second per user, and never sends the score. The lock screen runs the full scan only for a likely face, and that scan, with the liveness gate, decides (`auth/attempt.rs`: `Authenticator::probe`; Omarchy's lock `Service.qml`).
- **A touch while locked can only unlock.** With face and FIDO2 both set up, the lock screen's `omarchy-lock-fido2` stack is pam_faceauth's `key-unlock` line closed by `pam_deny`, never pam_u2f. The lock screen asks the key only while its displays are lit, as it scans the face: blanking ends the check, and a wake (a key, a click, a move, or the face probe finding the owner) asks again at once (round 19). The lane runs only while that file exists and the daemon's `key_lane` answer, asked without the camera, says the lane is there; once offered it stays for the whole lock, and only the daemon's own "not available" withdraws it, so a busy camera or a dropped reply does not take it away. With the daemon down (its socket missing, or nothing listening on it), `faceauth key-lane` answers "not running", and the lock screen drops the key lane, stops the face probe and scan, hides both, and says "Face and key are unavailable because faceauth isn't running. Use your password." until the next well-formed answer; a busy or unreadable answer changes nothing. The daemon serves a key unlock only to the lock screen's own caller (as the user, never root, for the caller's own name, in the active graphical session) and only while the compositor says Locked; it runs the same sandboxed check as the card, on sudo's key line's policy, user verification carried over and no lane at all for a line that asks for the PIN, and stops it at once when the lock screen hangs up, within a second of the unlock, and at its deadline. The lock screen's reserved channel (round 7: one long-lived connection on `/run/faceauth/lock.sock`, outside the connection limits, at most two per account, held for the whole lock), a key unlock's ask and the daemon's own lock each latch the user; the key-lane questions only show the lane. From then until the lock screen has closed its channel with its graceful "unlocked", the daemon has seen the session unlocked with no ask under way, and `key_unlock_hold_ms` (500 unless set) has passed, the card's key is disarmed, a touch it left is dropped, a check under way is stopped, none starts, a touch reported for one counts for nothing, no stack's key line runs, and every consent request reads the session as locked and parks, whatever the compositor answers. A touch that unlocked does not end the latch on its own, and neither does a channel that drops without the graceful word (the lock screen killed): the user stays latched until a later channel closes gracefully, or the anchored compositor (below) has said unlocked for 3 s from looks no more than 2 s apart. An open channel the anchored compositor contradicts for 10 s the same way expires: the daemon stops counting it, tells the lock screen's CLI so and closes it, which frees its reserved place, and the hold runs; the CLI then waits for the lock screen's word without a channel, so no other opens for that lock. A graceful close releases the latch only once that compositor says unlocked. A latch reads as held, the short wait after an unlock, only while its hold clock runs; a dropped channel's latch, or one whose unlock has not been seen, reads as unknown, which the password deadline bounds (round 8). Any account's key unlock takes the key from every account, once a fresh anchored look has found that account locked (an account that is not locked cannot keep anyone's key off with key-unlock asks): from then until that account's anchored unlock is seen and the hold after it, or until logind's record for the session its lock screen asked from (pinned when the priority starts, the one desktop resolved then) no longer says `ACTIVE=1` on a seat or is gone (a session with no seat, which logind always writes as active, never starts or holds it, the rule approvals use: fix round 13, Opus N3), whatever other sessions the account has (never on a timer, so a lock screen slow or suspended between retries does not end it), no account's card or key line checks the key and every account's check under way is stopped first, while their key lines go to the password within their budget, so one key registered to two accounts never lets one account's unlock touch approve another account's request (round 8). The unlock check stops the moment its session leaves the foreground (a switch to another account's VT), even while its lock question is out (that runs on a thread of its own, fix round 11, Codex 5), a touch counts only while it is still there, no other check starts before it has stopped, and a session in the background cannot start one, so another account's touch at its own lock screen never reaches the check of the session left behind (fix round 10). A locked account in the background asks no anchor at all: its lock screen's retries each cost one look at logind's record until it is in front again (fix round 11, Opus N3). The other way round holds too (fix round 11, Opus N2): every approval check, the card's key and the stacks' key lines alike, starts, runs and commits only while the request's own session (sudo's session, or from the user manager the user's one desktop) says `ACTIVE=1` on its seat in logind's record, asked once more after the commit's lock question, under the record's lock, right at the commit (fix round 12, Codex 1); a session with no seat (an SSH login), which logind always writes as active, counts only while the desktop its lock question goes to is in front on its seat (fix round 12, Opus N1); otherwise the key line waits, counted as unknown, and goes on to the password, so a sudo left running in an account switched away from, or in an SSH login, never takes the touch made at the screen in front. One key check runs at a time across processes: each holds the key's lease, an exclusive `flock` on `/run/faceauth-key.lease` (root's, 0600, made by the package's tmpfiles entry), from before it asks the key until it exits, whoever runs it, so a check of the daemon's and one of the module's during an outage never share the key; a check's unit stops with the daemon's service, when it stops, restarts or dies, and is never run again by it (`StopPropagatedFrom=faceauth.service`, fix round 12: `PartOf=` would rerun a live check with a crash's automatic restart), and the daemon stops any left from before when it starts (fix round 11, Codex 2, Opus N4). Each account's latches move only that account's epoch, so one account opening and closing its lock channel cannot refuse another account's commits; the priority starting and the daemon's own limit refusing a caller move every account's (fix round 10); a request one account holds to the request deadline without a line moves only its own (fix round 13, Codex 4). The latch, the key's commit and the key's consumption share the consent record's one lock. The card comes back after the unlock only on a fresh acknowledgment, never the one from before the lock, and needs its own answer (`consent/key.rs`: `KeyLane::unlock`; `consent/state.rs`: `ConsentState::latch_lock_screen`, `unlock_holds`, `observe_lock`; `server/admit.rs`: `admit_key_unlock`; `server/handle.rs`: `key_lane_reply`; `pam_faceauth/src/lib.rs`: `pam_code`; Omarchy's `shell/plugins/lock/Service.qml`, `install/helpers/pam-stack.sh`).
- **Every key use goes through faceauthd while face is set up.** Face setup turns Omarchy's pam_u2f line in sudo and polkit-1 into pam_faceauth's `key-fallback` line right below the face line, with exactly its `cue`, `pinverification=` and `userverification=` words (absent, `=0` and `=1` kept apart, each with its own private stack), and FIDO2 setup writes that line when face is set up (plain pam_u2f, as upstream, when it is not); face removal turns it back into the pam_u2f line with the same words and deletes the lock lane. Face setup never leaves a pam_u2f line above or beside face: a key line it cannot carry over exactly (a word it does not mirror, another key file or control, a word said twice, a second key line) stops it with status 2 before anything is installed or changed, naming the file and line and the fix, and no mode ever rewrites a key line with defaults it could not read. The daemon runs the line's check in the same sandboxed helper, exclusive per user with the card's check and the lock lane (the card on screen takes the key from it, the lock screen from both), for a root sudo or polkit caller only. While faceauthd runs, only the one lock verdict (`lockgate::is_locked`, in the daemon) lets the line run: it answers unlocked only when it can show it, and weighs the compositor's word, logind's records, the latch, the lock screen's channel, the daemon's start (everyone latched for one hold) and its connection places (a caller refused at the daemon's limit latches everyone for one hold, at an account's share that account, so filling them only denies service). In the daemon a lock is positive, and waited on without a deadline, only with the lock screen's channel open and the compositor saying locked; either without the other is unknown. While it reads anything else, or a card has the key, the line waits: the module says once "The screen is locked. Your security key will be offered after you unlock it." and asks again each second on a short connection, holding none of the daemon's 8 connection places between asks; without a deadline on a positive lock, and for at most 10 s in all on anything else (one wait budget per line, summed over every unknown and held wait and every check such a verdict cut off, and never reset, round 8), after which the line goes to the password. A request from a text console or a remote login never waits behind a lock it cannot see: it goes to the password at once. The line runs only once the daemon has seen the unlock and `key_unlock_hold_ms` has passed, and is stopped the moment the verdict leaves unlocked (within a second), a latch begins (at once) or a card arms, after which it waits again; a touch counts only if the verdict, asked again at the commit under the record's lock, still says unlocked. The lock screen's key lane comes first: its ask stops any account's approval check at once. With a PIN policy (the line's `pinverification=1`, or pam_u2f.conf's default where the line says nothing, read the way the daemon reads it; round 8) the module asks for the key's PIN on the PAM conversation (sudo's terminal, polkit's agent) only once the line may run, with no connection to the daemon open, so a slow PIN never runs into the daemon's 5-second request deadline, then passes it on a fresh connection for that one check, keeps it across a wait, and wipes it. pam_u2f's own defaults (`/etc/security/pam_u2f.conf`) count for every word the line leaves out; a file that sets anything else withholds the key. A credential registered with its PIN required (`+pin`) gets no lane unless the effective policy asks for the PIN, since pam_u2f asks it for its PIN whatever `pinverification=0` says, and no lane answers a prompt it did not ask for; doctor says to set `pinverification=1`. A text console's sudo takes the key only while logind's records show no graphical session of anyone's at all (a session counting as graphical by its type now or the type it was created with, which its controller cannot change), nothing is latched and the lock screen has not asked lately; with a desktop anywhere, the verdict must say unlocked (round 8). **With faceauthd down, the key is stock Omarchy's** (fix round 10, Mike's ruling). Down means the service is stopped or has failed for good: its runtime directory, `/run/faceauth`, is gone (systemd removes it once the unit is inactive or failed with no restart to come and no job pending, and keeps it through a crash's automatic restart and through `systemctl restart`, since the unit says `RuntimeDirectoryPreserve=restart`: v262 src/core/unit.c, `unit_release_resources`; src/core/service.c, `service_will_restart`). A socket missing or refusing inside a runtime directory that is still there is a daemon restarting, which goes to the password, never to the stock key (fix round 11), and so do a directory that cannot be looked at (only "not found" is gone, fix round 12), busy, a malformed reply, a peer that is not root and a timeout. While faceauthd is down, the key-fallback line behaves as stock Omarchy's pam_u2f line, unlocked, locked and at a text console alike: it runs pam_u2f through the package's `faceauth-keycheck --converse`, on the private stack that carries the line's own words, as root with a clean environment, no core dump and outside the daemon's sandbox, as stock pam_u2f runs inside sudo, with every message of pam_u2f's conversation passed through to the caller (its PIN prompt, once per credential that asks, a `+pin` credential included; its cue, when the line says `cue`), and none of the card's rules about credentials or pam_u2f's defaults, which pam_u2f reads itself (fix round 11, Codex 4). It asks nothing about the lock, logind's records or where the request comes from, and waits for no unlock, then goes on to the password. That includes stock's exposure: during an outage a sudo or polkit request hidden behind the lock screen can take a touch, as it can on stock Omarchy. It is accepted because an outage cannot be caused on demand, and the lock screen does not ask for the key while faceauthd is down (it says face and key are unavailable), so no touch is being asked for there. That rests on two things: code running as the user cannot crash or stop faceauthd (every read of what a user controls is capped before it allocates, `/proc/<pid>/cmdline` at 64 KiB among them, since checkpoint/restore lets a process point its arguments at any mapping of its own, kernel/sys.c `prctl_set_mm_map`; the kernel's OOM killer picks the daemon late, `OOMScoreAdjust=-500`), and a crash's restart is not an outage (above); a bug that broke the first would hand that code stock Omarchy's exposure only while the service stays failed. What still differs from stock: the touch window is 6 s, not the key's own timeout, and the time a prompt waits on the user does not count against it; the check is stopped within 250 ms of the daemon answering again, a prompt still open included (while a prompt waits on the user the check is watched on a thread of its own and killed then, and once the prompt returns the line goes to the password; otherwise it goes back to the daemon, which asks for a PIN again if its line needs one), and a touch counts only if the daemon is still down when it lands, so two checks never share the key; the check has a hard deadline of its own, 60 s with prompt time included, so a prompt left open (an unanswered PIN prompt, a polkit dialog) gives the key's lease back by then, and it runs in a session of its own, so the terminal's Ctrl-Z cannot freeze it (fix round 12, Codex 2, Opus N2); every check, the daemon's and this one, holds the key's lease, so one already running sends this one to the password; and it runs only on sudo's and polkit's stacks, for an account that is not root, with a helper root alone can write. The check answers over a pipe, its answers come back down a socket that it reads straight from its descriptor, a byte at a time, so each is taken as it comes (fix round 13, Opus N1: a buffered reader took a whole answer on its first byte and then waited for bytes it already held, so every PIN or cue line went to the password), and it is signalled and reaped only through a pidfd the child takes of itself between fork and exec and passes up before it runs anything (fix round 13, Codex 1), never by its pid, so a PAM caller that ignores SIGCHLD, or reaps its own children, neither hangs the line nor loses the answer, and no process that reused the pid is ever signalled. Nothing of the daemon's or its helpers' signals by pid either (`faceauth_daemon::child`). `faceauth doctor` says whether the path can run (`key.down`, `key.lease`, and `key.down_polkit` for polkit's helper sandbox, which needs pam-u2f's drop-in to reach the key, as stock pam_u2f does). So on sudo a no at a card you saw still goes on to the key and then the password, as ruled, and on polkit a no still ends the request. A pam_u2f line in sudo or polkit-1 added afterwards by hand reads the key outside the daemon: the daemon then offers the key nowhere (no card key, no lock lane, no key line), the next setup stops on it, and `faceauth doctor` names it (`pam.key_lines`) and says why on its key rows (`consent/key.rs`: `KeyLane::fallback`, `lane_policy`; `server/admit.rs`: `admit_key_fallback`; Omarchy's `install/helpers/pam-stack.sh`).
- **Elevation takes a nod at the daemon's own camera.** Nothing elevates until the card has acknowledged the request, the face has matched, the same followed face has nodded twice, and a strobed confirm and the nod frames match the templates. Two shakes refuse; the password typed into the card approves, and so does a touch of the user's security key (below). A nod approval commits only on a fresh anchored look, asked before the consent record's lock and judged under it with its epochs, as a key touch's commit is: a lock that lands during the nods, the confirm or the look itself parks the request instead (fix round 9). Between the nods and the commit the daemon tells the PAM line it is at work before each step (the key watch's end, the "Confirming" card, the confirm, the frames' match and the look), each shorter than the line's patience, so a slow step never sends a good nod to the password (fix round 10). No face in view, keypress, click or socket message stands in for the nod (`auth/consent_flow.rs`: `consent_round`; `auth/confirm.rs`: `confirm`, `nod_frames_match`). Nods count from the match, with no dwell after the nod card and no still second before the first leg (Mike's 10/19 ruling: finding the face takes long enough on its own); a card that names a passwordless rider keeps both, 1.5 seconds and a still second, however the rider reached it (re-shown after the nods began, or on the first nod show when armed just after the match), since no scan need come before its nods, and that card does not show the nod cue (`consent/state.rs`: `rider_on_card`; `consent/wait.rs`: `nod_detector`). Arming closes with the nods, in the same look that catches a rider armed since the window's last check, which sends the card back guarded before any confirm (`consent/state.rs`: `close_rider_arming`). What that leaves: a nod still in motion from approving one card can count toward a card queued behind it, where the change from one card to the next is plain on screen; and a person already nodding for another reason, a call or music, when a request appears can approve it once their face is matched, if the motion meets every rule below. The question whether the session locked under the card runs on a thread of its own every 2 seconds, so no frame waits for it; a watch that dies reads as locked (`consent/wait.rs`: `LockWatch`).
- **The card names the request.** For `sudo` and `pkexec` it shows the command line from `/proc` and the requester's process chain, stripped of control and direction-override characters, and cut with a flag past 16,384 characters. Another polkit action's requester is verified only from polkitd's process ids as the agent relays them, in a context tied to the request by polkitd's cookie, and otherwise reads "Unverified:" with nothing to kill (`consent/requester.rs`: `CallerInfo::from_pid`, `polkit_requester`). The module asks the agent for the cookie with a fresh random challenge and takes only an answer that carries it, so text typed into that question, were an agent to show it by mistake, is never taken for a cookie (`lockgate::polkit_cookie_answer`). Deny and kill sends the no, then asks `faceauth kill-requester` to end the process the daemon pinned for the card, named by pid and pidfs id, so a pid another process holds by then is never signalled; with no pinned process the card offers no kill (`CallerInfo::pin_kill`, `kill_pinned`, round 19). Any close of a card says no while its request waits, whoever closed it: the Dismiss button, Escape, the shell hiding it, or the daemon's own hide after a no or a requester gone (a no the daemon then refuses or counts as the same one). A closed card lets go of its request, so the next request's card is shown, and a late payload for the closed one changes nothing.
- **Gestures are head angles** from MediaPipe's face mesh, without which the daemon will not start. A gesture is four alternating legs, each within 0.6 seconds (a shake's from a head still for a second before), with the face box moving along the axis, so a frozen box or a flipped fit cannot nod, and a glance, a look down, reading, talking or leaning in reads as nothing (`consent/detector.rs`: `NodDetector`, `ShakeDetector`; `consent/round.rs`: tests `mesh_battery_holds`, `mesh_redteam`).
- **Each person's floors come from their own rounds.** A floor is 0.85 of the lowest at which the person's recorded nods, or shakes, still read, kept within 8 to 16 degrees for a nod and 15 to 24 for a shake. It is then raised a degree at a time until none of their everyday rounds reads as that gesture, up to 60 and 80. A raise past their own gesture switches that gesture off with a warning, and an everyday round that reads at the ceiling fails the session (`store/templates.rs`: `GestureCal::floors_deg`; `enrol/everyday.rs`: `clear_everyday`).
- **No deadline, no unattended nod loop.** Nods are read for `consent_seconds` (90) after a match and then disarmed. They re-arm only when your face, checked to be yours, has been away and is back, on Ready to nod, or on a face unlock. A face that merely stays in view does not re-arm them. A waiting card looks every 2 seconds, or every `battery_tick_seconds` (10) on battery, and identifies every look in secure mode. With the walk-away lock on and an away time in force, its away clock follows the watch's rules for the mode, and a user gone locks the session until a face unlock resumes the request. Otherwise it waits. A lock under the card parks it. A card the daemon stops re-sending hides without answering. Requests queue one at a time, and approvals, refusals and waiting requests post a notice (`auth/rearm.rs`: `attention_wait`, `UserSeen`; `server/rounds.rs`: `consent_rounds`).
- **The answer token buys no approval.** A process running as you can read it and use it only to acknowledge the card, dismiss the request, re-arm the nods (Ready to nod), submit one password for PAM to check, or ask for the passwordless rider on a sudo request while your face is in the nod window. It cannot answer for the key. It reaches the card on the stdin of the resolved Omarchy tree's `omarchy-shell -s` and the shell's own socket, never a command line (a tree without `-s` shows no card rather than put it there), so no process list or `/proc/PID/cmdline` shows it; a process running as you can still read the card's memory. A rider armed that way re-shows the card naming it and restarts the acknowledgment, the dwell and the nods (`consent/shell.rs`: `shell_summon`; `server/handle.rs`: `handle`; `consent/state.rs`: `ConsentState::answer`, `ConsentState::arm_passwordless`).
- **The security key on the card is the touch the stack already takes.** While the nods are armed (the scan and the nod window, never a hold or the wait for attention), the daemon asks the user's FIDO2 key through pam_u2f against `/etc/fido2/fido2`, with what the lane's own key-fallback line in `/etc/pam.d/sudo` or `polkit-1` asks of the key mirrored into a private stack under `/usr/lib/faceauth/pam.d`: `pinverification=` (`=1`, for which the card asks the PIN) and `userverification=`, each absent, `=0` or `=1` as the line has it. A line with any other word or control, a word said twice, or a second one, keeps the key off the card for that lane, and any pam_u2f line in either stack keeps it off every lane, and `faceauth doctor` says why on its `key.sudo`, `key.polkit` and `key.unlock` rows (`pam.lock_fido2` checks the lock screen's stack). The key is offered only for a user the key file names. The check runs in a transient unit of its own: root with no capabilities, no network and no AF_UNIX socket, systemd's, D-Bus's and udev's sockets hidden besides, a read-only system and /dev, the hidraw nodes as its only devices (so a key is reached over USB alone: libfido2's NFC backend, which needs AF_NFC sockets, does not run there, and an NFC-only key is not offered through face authentication), no other process in sight, the daemon's store, socket, config and sudoers out of reach, and a 30-second runtime cap. A stop is SIGTERM, which the helper answers by cancelling the key's wait with libfido2's own cancel and exiting, so the key goes dark with the check instead of blinking on to its own timeout and answering the next check busy; whatever has not ended a second later is killed (round 19). The helper exports libfido2's `fido_dev_get_assert` so that pam_u2f's call to the key reaches a wrapper that notes the request in flight and passes every argument and answer through untouched (`keycheck_cancel.rs`); `faceauth doctor`'s `key.cancel` row checks both ends, the helper's export and pam_u2f's import. Its exit status is the only answer, and a stopped check never reads as a touch. The PIN reaches it down a pipe, never argv or the environment, is wiped like the password, is never logged, serves one check, and answers only the first PIN prompt of that check, so a wrong one costs the key one retry. A touch counts only for the watch generation that is armed now on the request whose card armed it, for a check that started a dwell (1.5 s) after the card's latest acknowledgment, while no rider waits to be shown, never over an answer the card already gave, never from a watch that is ending, only while no lock-screen latch holds the user, and only when the compositor says, under the record's lock at the commit, that the session is unlocked: no answer, an error or a timeout counts as locked. A rider armed mid-check (marked shown and the acknowledgment reset in one step), a re-shown card, a hold, a lock, a park, a key unlock from the lock screen, or the unlock's re-show (which drops the old acknowledgment and waits for a fresh one; a card that does not come back ends the request unapproved) stops the check. A PIN spent on a check has the card ask for it again, during the scan as in the nod window. Approved that way, the request carries a rider the card showed, as a nod's does. A key that would approve without a touch is on no lane at all: a credential registered without `+presence` (unless pam_u2f.conf says `userpresence=1`), or a key line or pam_u2f.conf with `userpresence=0`, withholds the key from the card, the key lines and the lock screen, and doctor says why. The check is stopped before the request's answer goes back to PAM, so the key is free for the stack's key-fallback line after a refusal on sudo. A touch leaves the faillock counter alone, as pam_u2f on sudo's stack does. A USB device that takes over libfido2 in the check can still answer yes for the request it was asked about, as it could in sudo's own pam_u2f; it reaches nothing of the daemon's (`consent/key.rs`: `KeyLane::watch`, `lane_policy`; `consent/state.rs`: `ConsentState::key_touched`, `rider_snapshot`; `consent/anchor.rs`: `anchored_lock_state`; `pamcheck.rs`: `conv_pin`; `keycheck.rs`).
- **The fingerprint on the card is the finger the stack already takes, and rides with the key** (Mike's 10/12 ruling). While the nods are armed, and every lid is not shut (a laptop's reader is under its lid), the daemon asks the user's fingerprint through pam_fprintd from a private stack under `/usr/lib/faceauth/pam.d`, `faceauth-fprint` (`auth required pam_fprintd.so max-tries=3 timeout=110`: pam_fprintd's own three tries, and a per-try timeout that covers the card's whole armed window as shipped, the 20 s scan and the 90 s nod window, so the reader is claimed once per window rather than every two seconds as the key is asked), against the prints `sudo fprintd-enroll` stored. A matching finger approves as the key's touch does, under the same commit (`ConsentState::fprint_matched`): only for the armed generation of the request whose card armed it, for a check that started a dwell after the card's latest acknowledgment, while no rider waits, never over an answer the card already gave, only while no latch or unlock priority holds the user, with the request's own session in front and the one verdict, asked afresh at the commit, saying unlocked. Its answer is its own (`Answer::Fingerprint`), never the key's. Three wrong fingers end the fingerprint for that window, and the card's line goes. The card says one of three things beside it: place a finger, no match (until the check ends), or the reader is busy. The fingerprint is offered only with fprintd installed and a finger enrolled for the user (counted under `/var/lib/fprint`, which only root reads); a pam_fprintd line in sudo's or polkit's stack, or in a stack either reaches through its `include` and `substack` lines (and `@include`), takes the reader outside the daemon, and a finger meant for the lock screen could then approve a request hidden behind it, so with one there no lane offers the fingerprint, as a pam_u2f line keeps the key off. The includes are followed at most 8 deep and 32 files in all, by plain names in `/etc/pam.d` only; one that is missing, names a path, or nests past that counts as unknown and withholds the fingerprint too (`consent/fprint.rs`: `fprint_offer`, `fprintd_reached`). The check is `faceauth-fprintcheck` in a transient unit of its own: root with no capabilities, no network, AF_UNIX alone, no device at all (`PrivateDevices=`, `DevicePolicy=closed`: fprintd owns the reader), no other process in sight, the daemon's store, socket, config, sudoers and the key file out of reach, systemd's and udev's control sockets hidden, and a 340 s runtime cap. Unlike the key's it reaches the system bus, which it needs to talk to fprintd, and root with no capability can still ask systemd for anything there. **pam_fprintd hears its result over that bus, and on a stock bus anyone can forge it.** Upstream pam_fprintd takes the verify result from a signal it matches by fprintd's bus name, sd-bus does not hold a signal sent straight to it to that name's owner (systemd src/libsystemd/sd-bus/bus-match.c, the sender match), and the system bus's default policy lets any connection send any other a signal (`/usr/share/dbus-1/system.conf`): so a program running as any user can hand a waiting pam_fprintd a match that never came from the reader. The weakness is upstream's and reaches stock pam_fprintd lines too (sudo's, polkit's, the lock screen's). The package's bus policy, `/usr/share/dbus-1/system.d/faceauth-fprint.conf` (`packaging/system.d/faceauth-fprint.conf`), closes it: a signal on fprintd's device interface reaches a receiver only from the connection that owns fprintd's name, which only root may own (fprintd's own bus policy). The rules are in the mandatory context, which the bus applies after every user and group context, so another package's root policy that allowed receiving signals broadly cannot undo them for the root receivers that matter (sudo's stack, the check); a rule in the default context could be undone that way, measured. It protects every pam_fprintd on a machine with the package installed, stock lines included, and pacman's dbus-reload hook reloads the bus when the file lands. The fingerprint is offered, on the card and the lock screen, only while that file stands as shipped and root's alone (`consent/fprint.rs`: `bus_policy_state`), and doctor's `fprint.bus_policy` says whether it does. What remains: root can still send such a signal, and needs none; a bus that has not read the file since it landed does not have it; and the rules are tested on dbus-daemon only (`faceauth-daemon/tests/fprint_virtual.rs`: a forged match approves with the system's default policy alone, and approves nothing with the file, also beside a user policy that allows every signal), while on dbus-broker, Arch's default bus, its policy parser takes these rules but enforcement has not been tested. A stop is SIGTERM, which ends pam_fprintd at once (it catches only SIGINT), and fprintd cancels the verify and closes the reader when the check's bus name goes (fprintd src/device.c, `_fprint_device_client_vanished`), so no cancel wrapper is needed; SIGKILL follows a second later. Its exit status is the only answer (42 a match, 3 the reader did not answer, 1 anything else), a stopped check never reads as a match, and its status words (`place`, `retry`) only change the card's line. fprintd lets one client claim a reader, so the daemon hands it over through the key's own arbiter (`KeyLane::claim_reader`, one fingerprint check at a time, whoever's): the lock screen comes before the card, the card before any fallback line. **A finger while locked can only unlock.** With face set up and the fingerprint going through the daemon, the lock screen's `omarchy-lock-fingerprint` stack is pam_faceauth's `fprint-unlock` line with `[success=done ignore=ignore module_unknown=ignore default=die]`, then stock Omarchy's pam_fprintd line; the line's `module_unknown=ignore` sends a stack whose pam_faceauth.so is gone (the package removed) to that stock line too, where `default=die` alone would fail it (Linux-PAM 1.7.3 answers `PAM_MODULE_UNKNOWN` for a module that will not load, measured). The daemon serves it as it serves the key's unlock, to the lock screen's own caller, only while the anchored compositor says Locked, from a session in front, and with the key's latch and shared priority: its ask takes the card off the key and the reader in one step, a card's check under way is stopped before this one claims the reader, and the latch holds until the daemon has seen the unlock and the hold has passed (`consent/fprint.rs`: `FprintLane::unlock`; `consent/state.rs`: `begin_fprint_unlock`). Its ask is counted apart from the key's, so the two lanes the lock screen runs side by side never refuse each other. The module passes the check's status to the lock screen's conversation as pam_fprintd's own messages would come. **With faceauthd down, the lock screen's fingerprint is stock Omarchy's** (Mike's 10/12 ruling): down as the key-fallback line reads it (the runtime directory gone), the `fprint-unlock` line answers PAM_IGNORE and the stock pam_fprintd line below it runs as the user; a daemon restarting, and every other failure, is PAM_AUTH_ERR, and the lock screen's lane shows from the daemon's `fprint_lane` answer while it runs. `faceauth doctor` says what stands (`fprint.service`, `fprint.unlock`, `pam.fprint_lines`, `fprint.stray`, which follows the same includes as the offer, and `fprint.bus_policy`). The card's check and the lock screen's run in units of their own (`faceauth-fprint-card-<user>.service`, `faceauth-fprint-unlock-<user>.service`), so a stop that failed on one does not leave the other's name taken. Not built yet: the polkit rule that keeps fprintd to root while faceauth runs (until it is, a program running as you can claim the reader so the card says busy, or take a finger placed for the card, which approves nothing for it), the fallback line after a decline on sudo, and Omarchy's setup and removal for the fingerprint; until setup moves the fingerprint into the daemon, its stock lines in sudo and polkit-1 keep the fingerprint off the card and the lock screen on its stock lane (`fprint.stray` names them).
- **Passwordless sudo from the card** is the one way elevation becomes passive. The approval that grants a request can also write Omarchy's `/etc/sudoers.d/99-omarchy-nopasswd-<user>` and its removal timer for up to 24 hours, while any process running as you uses sudo freely. The rider dies with its request, and is refused on polkit and while the daemon is not following the enrolled face, when the card grays it. It is the only file the daemon writes outside `/var/lib/faceauth` and `/run/faceauth` (`consent/passwordless.rs`: `enable_passwordless`).
- **Every failure falls back to the password.** On a look the module answers `PAM_IGNORE` to all but a match. On a consent line it answers `PAM_AUTH_ERR` to your no from polkit's helper only (a shake, a dismissal, a refused confirm), so the `polkit-1` line's `auth_err=die` ends the stack, the agent cancels the request, and neither the key nor a password dialog follows. On `sudo` the same no, like every other unapproved end while the session reads as unlocked, is `consent_denied`, which the module ignores, so sudo's key-fallback line and then its own prompt follow. Every consent reply passes one gate (`lane_answer`) on its way out: while the verdict reads anything but unlocked, a sudo request's unapproved end is `refused_parked`, and a polkit request's a refusal that ends its stack only on a positive lock with the request from the locked desktop; otherwise polkit too goes on to its key line and its password. The module answers `PAM_AUTHINFO_UNAVAIL` to `refused_parked`, to the daemon's error and to every failure to reach the daemon at all (no daemon, the connection cap's `busy`, a peer that is not root, a reply broken off or malformed, 6 s with no word from a daemon that should be at work, a panic), on sudo and polkit alike; under Omarchy's controls that goes on, as an ignore does, to the key-fallback line, which goes through the daemon too and waits while locked, so nothing a consent line leaves reaches the key while the screen is locked and faceauthd is running; with faceauthd down the key line is stock Omarchy's (above). A key line (`key-fallback`, `key-unlock`) answers `PAM_AUTH_ERR` to anything but a touch, a panic included, and a key-unlock line to no daemon as well; the lock screen's `fprint-unlock` line answers `PAM_IGNORE` only with faceauthd down, for the stock fingerprint line below it, and `PAM_AUTH_ERR` to anything else but a match. The module never sees a password; it converses only to ask a key-fallback line's PIN, to say to touch the key, to say once that the key waits for the unlock, and to pass the fingerprint check's status to the lock screen (`pam_faceauth/src/lib.rs`: `pam_code`; `auth/outcome.rs`: `lane_answer`, `refused`; `server/handle.rs`: `handle`).
- **The password stays in reach.** No faceauth failure, alone or combined, keeps sudo or polkit from the password line for more than about 15 s, unless the screen is positively locked and the request comes from the locked desktop itself, or the user is at work on a card or a PIN prompt. One exception is the user's own no: for a minute after a shake, a dismissal or a refused confirm (doubling with each further no within 10 minutes, to 8 minutes at most), a polkit request is refused without a card or a password dialog, as the no itself was, so a program cannot ask again and again until a password is typed; sudo, whose prompt is the asking program's own, goes on to its key line and password (`server::backoff`, `handle.rs`: the standing refusal). Waits on a card on screen are free only for 10 s in all and then count like an unknown wait, so cards shown one after another cannot hold a key line off the password either. The daemon tells a waiting consent or key line it is still at work every 2 s, from inside the request's own wait loops, and the module gives up on 6 s of silence, so a hung daemon costs the face line and the key line 6 s each at most; a dead one hands the key line to the stock key check, which gives the touch 6 s. A lock state nobody can read (Hyprland missing, a field renamed, a second instance directory, no compositor logind names or two of them, the lock screen's channel and the compositor disagreeing, a channel dropped or a stale second one left open, or channels opened and closed over and over) is waited on for 10 s at most in all: a parked consent request and the key line each keep one wait budget, summed over every wait on a verdict that is neither unlocked nor a positive lock and every check such a verdict cut off, never reset, and a consent request that spends its budget sends the key line on to the password at once (round 8); a slow bus, and a listener that never accepts on Hyprland's socket or the daemon's are no answer within 1 s (every connect is non-blocking, under one deadline); a key check waits for a touch 6 s; logind's records unreadable count as a desktop, so the compositor decides; a PAM caller with SIGCHLD ignored still gets the key check's answer; a camera held elsewhere is waited on for 4 s, then the request answers busy; every call into the user's session (the card's summon, a notice, the lock helper's calls) is read for a bounded time (4.5 s, the helper's 11 s) and given up with what it started killed, never read to the end of its pipes, which the units hold, one read per stream per pass with the deadline checked every pass, so writers that keep a pipe full cannot outrun it (fix round 11, Opus N1, Codex 7), and both of its units end themselves at 4 s (`RuntimeMaxSec=`, with `KillSignal=SIGKILL`, so a command that ignores SIGTERM does not act late, fix round 11, Codex 6), so a wedged manager or same-user code on its bus cannot hold a request; a request queued behind requests whose cards never show (a summon stalled) gives up after 7.5 s and its key line goes on to the password at once, while a wait behind a card on screen is the user's (fix round 10). Each of these has a test that drives the module's real control flow against fakes and reaches the password in time, and a test at the real scale holds the bounds to the 15 s (`pam_faceauth/src/lib.rs`: `key_line`, `ask_daemon`, `run_converse`, `connect_to`; `lockgate.rs`: `WaitBudget`, `anchored`; `server/rounds.rs`: `Park`, `unknown_too_long`). The key-fallback line's 6 s touch window is shorter than stock pam_u2f's wait for the key's own timeout: a missed touch gets the password prompt.

- **Enrollment takes one live face and saves once.** It is root only, reads one face per frame, follows the face its first still look learned, and after a lost track counts nothing until that face matches again. A strobed pair must pass the flash gate at the center and each hold (each terminal look), so it needs the strobe control unless `liveness_required` is off. New templates must match the session's face, straight ones at the accept threshold and turned ones within 0.30 (provisional, not measured). Low turned ones are dropped, and a straight miss, over half of one look, three in a row or a quarter of the set fails it. The one save comes at a successful end, so Cancel stores nothing, and the camera is off on the Welcome and Bridge screens (`enrol/follow.rs`: `new_set_is_the_face`; `enrol/flash.rs`: `check`; `enrol/walk.rs`: `run`; `enrol/terminal.rs`: `run`).
- **No enrollment checks the face against the account's stored looks.** Add Look and the terminal enrollments add whoever sits for them. The check waits on a measurement of how the model scores half faces.
- **A photo on a phone** was never seen as a face in the tests made, since the glass mirrors the illuminator and a display emits almost no near infrared. A video on a display the IR camera sees is not measured.
- **A paper print** is refused by two strobed cues: it reflects the flash several times more than skin, and its surround lights up with it. A pair whose flash clips is discarded, so a clipped print is never accepted on reflectance and is refused on it only once the exposure comes down. A waggled print that nods fails the confirm (`faceauth-engine/src/liveness.rs`: `FlashResponse`; `auth/confirm.rs`: `confirm`).
- **Replay.** Each attempt draws a random strobe mask (68 patterns, nine classes), scores only frames that follow it and redraws after each match, so a recording that ignores the strobe passes about one attempt in seventy. The sensor's walk order, bit 7 first, is measured. A scan that never follows the mask is logged, not charged. A pair whose exposure or gain reads back other than written is refused, and one that fails to read back is never passed. A device that replays a face in step with the control is not caught (`faceauth-engine/src/liveness.rs`: `StrobePhase`; `strobe.rs`: `StrobeGate`; `auth/attempt.rs`: `unscored`).
- **Walk-away lock** (opt-in, `sudo faceauth presence on`). The default mode looks every 10 seconds, lets any face turned to the screen hold it off, checks identity every third look only to report who holds it, and locks after `away_seconds` (20, or never) with nobody there. Secure mode looks every 5 seconds (10 on battery) and identifies each look; only the enrolled face holds it off, the first clear miss by a face turned to the screen locks at once, and an empty chair locks after `secure_away_seconds` (20, always a number). In both, a look after a sighting keeps trying for up to 2 seconds before it finds the chair empty. A print that fails the gate is never the user, and under the flash a print, phone or screen shows no face, so it holds neither mode, as tested live. A hidden face (a hand over it, a look down, a near miss) holds while the shape under the last face box is unchanged, for the obscured face lock time (never by default, 2 minutes in secure, 1 to 10). A mode switch lasts until the service restarts (`presence/watch.rs`: `Watch::step`, `tick_for`). With no graphical session of the user's in logind's records the watch pauses (no look, no lock) and resumes when one appears, and a lock that fails waits before the next try, doubling from the tick to 10 minutes. Nothing the daemon runs in the user's manager (the walk-away lock, the card's summon, a notice) runs without such a session: and the route into the manager can never start one anyway: a system unit runs as the user with no PAM session and calls `systemd-run --user` over the user's own bus socket, which exists only while the manager runs (`systemd-run --machine=USER@.host` is not used: it opens a PAM session, for which logind starts the manager). Every dispatch checks first that the sessions pinned when the work began (the card's when it is made, a request's notices when the request starts, an enrollment's when it starts, the walk-away lock's when it starts) are still the user's live desktops, and both units of each call check them again the moment they start, the carrier before it reaches the manager and the user's unit before the command, so a call a manager starts late runs nothing once its session is gone (fix round 10). What remains is a window of milliseconds between the user's unit's check and its command acting, against a logout and a fresh login in between, and same-user code, which owns the manager and the bus and can run anything it likes there anyway. Whether a walk-away lock happened is the anchored question's to say, never the manager's (`presence/run.rs`: `Governor`; `consent/shell.rs`: `user_command`, `Pin`; `presence/lock.rs`: `lock_session_at`).
- **Bad-password lockouts.** A face match clears the `pam_faillock` counter, as a correct password would (`auth/attempt.rs`: `faillock_reset`).
- **Remote callers.** A caller the daemon cannot show to be local (an `sshd` ancestor, a remote logind session, anything unverifiable) is refused before any card or camera, so a request typed into an ssh session never reaches the camera. On polkit the caller judged is the agent that connected the helper, pinned by pidfd, and polkitd's named process as well. A process your session services start for a remote shell (`systemd-run --user`, D-Bus activation, a user timer) counts as local and can raise a card, which the nod still stands behind (`server/locality.rs`: `locality`).
- **Root peers are trusted.** An effective-uid-0 peer may ask about any user, enroll, delete and calibrate. The PAM module builds only the plain look, the consent request, the key fallback, the key fallback's ask whether it may run, the key unlock and the fingerprint unlock, pinned by tests, and root is never authenticated by face (`server/handle.rs`: `handle`; `pam_faceauth/src/lib.rs`: `request_line`).
- **Rate limit.** Five failed attempts in a minute with a face seen, at the lock screen and the card together, start a 30-second hold. Repeats double it up to 8 minutes, and a match or 10 quiet minutes resets it. A hold refuses at the lock screen and pauses face checks at the card, whose scan scores a face no longer than a lock-screen attempt. After your no, requests are refused without a card for a minute, doubling up to 8 (`auth/cooldown.rs`: `Strikes`; `server/backoff.rs`: `refusal_standing`).
- **Templates** are 512-number embeddings, never images, root-only in `/var/lib/faceauth`, sealed to the TPM as a root-scoped systemd credential, or plaintext with a `doctor` warning on a machine without one. They are bound to the sensor's firmware node and the account's uid. There is no recovery key; what cannot be unsealed is re-enrolled (`store/files.rs`: `Store`; `store/seal.rs`: `Sealing`; `capture.rs`: `IrCapture`).
- **Connection limits** count as systemd counts a socket's (`server/limits.rs`): `max_connections` (64) in all and a share per account, `max_connections_per_source` (unset: the limit over the enrolled users, never under 16). A connection is charged to the account behind it: a user peer's uid; sudo's login uid, only where its real uid and its cgroup's `user-UID.slice` name the same account (a process whose login uid is unset may set it to any uid with no privilege, kernel/audit.c `audit_set_loginuid_perm`, so the login uid alone never picks the share; round 8), read through the peer's pidfd; polkit's helper's connecting uid (from its systemd instance name, believed only for a process in the system manager's own `system.slice/system-polkit\x2dagent\x2dhelper.slice`, read from the hierarchy's root, since a unit of the user's own may carry any name and a real sudo run in one would otherwise be charged to the uid the name claims, fix round 13, Codex 5; only PID 1 makes cgroups there and writes the name, and the process must also have real uid 0 and the helper's comm, `polkit-agent-he`, both readable without ptrace rights. Its executable is never asked: faceauthd is uid 0 with no capability and the helper runs as root with its unit's full permitted set, so the exe link, which takes ptrace access, never reads, security/commoncap.c `cap_ptrace_access_check`, and asking it sent every polkit request to the password; fix round 14, Opus N1, Codex 2; the request's lane, which picks polkit's card text and polkit-1's key policy, is decided by the same check, so a program run under the helper's name, sudo through a link included, keeps its own name as its lane, shows its own command and gets no key line, fix round 15, Opus N1); and unattributed root, which also takes anything that does not corroborate, a share of its own. A refusal at an account's share latches that account's key approvals for a hold, a refusal at the daemon's limit everyone's, and each is logged with its source and limit. A request held to the request deadline without a line latches its own account alone, as a refusal at its share does; unattributed root's latches nobody (fix round 13, Codex 4: charged to everyone, thirteen staggered stalls under one account's share kept every account's card and key off). The lock screen's channel is outside both.
- **The socket** is open to root and enrolled users only (0660 and a per-uid ACL), and so is the lock screen's channel socket beside it. Scores go to root callers only, and to the journal only at debug level. The `faceauth` command sends nothing to a listener that is not root (`server/acl.rs`: `apply_socket_acl`; `server/client.rs`: `send`).
- **Lid closed.** A consent request with the lid closed is answered before the camera is taken, and the stack goes on to its key-fallback line and the password (`server/admit.rs`: `lid_closed`).
- **The sandbox limits accidents, not a compromised daemon.** The unit's hardening, 1 GB memory cap and 666-task cap narrow what a bug can reach, and what the daemon reads of anything a user controls is capped before it allocates (fix round 11, Codex 1: `/proc` files at 64 KiB for a command line, 16 KiB for `status` and `cgroup`, less for the rest, a command line cut there shown cut on the card; requests and channel lines at 4 KiB; calls into the session at 4 KiB of output). The daemon still runs as root; a dedicated user with a small root helper is not built (`packaging/faceauth.service`).
- **The lock question is anchored to the genuine compositor** (round 8). The daemon asks only the compositor logind names as the controller of the session the request is bound to: sudo's own logind session, or the session of the agent that connected polkit's helper (the agent's pid from systemd's instance name, checked against its pidfs inode, and only for polkit's helper in its own slice, root with the helper's comm; anything else named as a helper has no binding, fix rounds 13 and 14); the user's only desktop for a request from neither, or from a text console. An answer is given only while the uid has exactly one controlled graphical session (greeters and closing sessions aside) and the binding names it: logind lets a session's owner take control of a session nobody controls and retype it graphical (systemd's `TakeControl` and `SetType`, as Hyprland started from a text console does), so a second one, made from a text console or an SSH login while the desktop is up, leaves nothing to answer, as do two desktops, a polkit agent of another account, and a binding that cannot be read. The controller's bus name is resolved to a pid on the system bus. Under `/run/user/UID/hypr/` at most 64 entries are looked at, each socket opened one path component at a time with `openat` and `O_NOFOLLOW`, its mount checked at every step (no symlink anywhere, no mount crossed; not `openat2`, which systemd blocks with ENOSYS in a dynamic user's unit) and checked on its descriptor to be a socket the user owns, then connected through /proc/self/fd without blocking, at most eight connections, every look into the directory on a guarded thread, all under one deadline; only the socket whose listener is that pid is used. Its reply is taken only when the kernel names that pid as the writer of every part (SO_PASSCRED and SCM_CREDENTIALS, which a writer can forge only with CAP_SYS_ADMIN over its pid namespace), so a process that steals a copy of the compositor's socket (pidfd_getfd, where ptrace rules allow it) writes as itself and is refused; the reader, the unprivileged helper, makes that check on the kernel's word before it parses a byte. The daemon runs this in a transient system unit, as the key check runs (`faceauth-lockstate --anchor UID BIND`, `consent/anchor.rs`): a dynamic user holding CAP_DAC_OVERRIDE alone, which it needs to reach the user's 0700 runtime directory and to connect to Hyprland's 0755 socket (a connect needs write permission on the socket, which CAP_DAC_READ_SEARCH does not grant), with no new privileges, a read-only file system, an empty /run and /run/user with only logind's session records, the system bus socket and this user's runtime directory bound back in, read-only (so no other control socket, /run/systemd/private among them, is reachable), AF_UNIX only, no network or devices, the daemon's state, config, the key file and sudoers out of sight, and, since CAP_DAC_OVERRIDE bypasses read checks too, the password and group shadows, SSH's keys, /boot, /efi, /var/lib and /var/log as well (keycheck's unit hides the same: root with no capability still owns them), and a 3 s runtime cap; it clears its ambient capabilities before it runs `busctl`, checks they are clear, so the child gets none, and drops the capability for good (`capset` to empty, read back) before it reads a byte from the compositor, answering nothing if either fails. The daemon takes its answer only as an exact pair of exit status and byte (0 unlocked, 1 locked), reads the byte as it comes with a deadline rather than waiting for the end of a pipe PID 1 holds, and shares a polled answer for up to 1 s from when its look began (concurrent polls of one session wait for the one look under way, and an older look never overwrites a newer answer); the commits (a nod's, the card's key, the key line's, and the unlock lane's lock check) always ask afresh, and ask before they take the consent record's lock. faceauthd itself keeps no capability. A stand-in listener on Hyprland's socket, a second or headless Hyprland, and the user manager's environment are never asked, and a graceful channel close releases the latch only once that compositor says unlocked. **Forging an unlock needs code inside the genuine compositor**, the process logind names as the session's controller: a Hyprland plugin, or `hyprctl eval`, which runs Lua inside the compositor, standard libraries loaded, for any client of its command socket; Hyprland's permission system does not gate it, `ecosystem:enforce_permissions` on or off (Hyprland 0.56.2, `debug/HyprCtl.cpp` `evalRequest`). That is the boundary.
- **Not defended**: code running inside the genuine compositor, which can make it say unlocked. That code is one command away for a process running as you (`hyprctl eval`, above). Short of that, a process running as you can still hide or disguise an armed card (cover it, or send it to a screen you are not watching), and a key touch or a finger meant for something else, a browser's key prompt say, then approves the hidden request; the nods need you facing the camera, but the key and the fingerprint arm with them for the nod window. Omarchy keeps the card out of screen captures (`no_screen_share` on its layer), so no check can read the card back from the screen. With it, a process running as you can put a card on screen behind the lock, and, by also forging the lock screen's goodbye (killing the channel, then opening and gracefully closing one of its own, as the same uid), have a touch of the key while locked approve that card. The lock screen is not a boundary against same-user code either: Omarchy's shell loads third-party plugins from your own config into its process. With faceauthd down the key line is stock Omarchy's pam_u2f line, which has no lock gate at all (fix round 10, Mike's ruling; above). Also not defended: a look-alike, a 3D mask, malware running as you with your password, a video on a display the IR camera sees, a camera that replays in step with the strobe, a process holding the IR capture node where face setup could not lock it (since fix round 11, Mike's ruling, setup locks the node faceauth reads its IR frames from to root, `/etc/udev/rules.d/72-faceauth-ir-node.rules` from `faceauth cam ir-rule`, but only when faceauth's own detection says it carries infrared alone, the node pinned is the one whose formats were read (the configured path made canonical, and the class entry's device number that of the opened node, fix round 12), and sysfs names attributes to pin it by: a CIO2 port by its name and the IPU3's PCI function, a USB camera by its interface on one rule line and its port and ids on the next, since two of one camera's interfaces can each have capture index 0 and udev matches one line's parent keys on one ancestor alone; otherwise it stays open, `camera.ir_capture` says why; face removal deletes the rule, gives the node it checks back what udev and logind give a capture node, root's, the video group's and no ACL (`faceauth cam ir-reset`, through a descriptor opened without following a link, on that node alone), and replays add events, since only an add sets a video node's group and uaccess never redoes an entry already there, so the seat user's access comes back whole, and `camera.ir_capture` fails while a lock outlives its rule (fix round 13, Codex 6, Opus nit); face falls to the password while another process holds it, and the holder can plausibly take unlit IR frames), setuid-root binaries you can run, and an Omarchy tree dev-linked from your home directory, which anything running as you can write.
- **The false-accept rate is unmeasured.** The 0.70 threshold was set on one subject and one print, and no other person's face has been scored against it. `required_matches` (2) is a spike filter, not a false-accept control (`config.rs`: `Config`).
- **No trusted-path marker.** A process running as you can draw a fake card and collect a password typed into it. Type one only for a request you just made, and prefer the nod, which gives a fake card nothing.
- **The polkit card needs Quickshell changes no package carries.** Quickshell 0.3.1, which Omarchy installs, neither passes a request's details to the agent nor serializes overlapping requests, and no release or package has either change. Without them every polkit card reads "Unverified:" with nothing to kill, and two overlapping requests leave the second hanging until killed, which can crash the shell that draws the bar and the lock screen (Quickshell issue #1016, open fix in PR #800). Neither grants anything, and `sudo` does not use the agent.
- **The lock screen runs no account check.** Quickshell's PAM context calls `pam_authenticate` and ends the transaction without `pam_acct_mgmt`, so the `account` lines of every lock screen stack (password, fingerprint, face and FIDO2 alike) never run: an expired account or a time or access restriction set there does not keep the lock screen shut. It is upstream Quickshell behavior and not fixed here; sudo and polkit run their account stacks as usual.

The review documents live with the project's design notes, outside this repository; the numbers below are theirs.

- **2026-09-21, 0.1.1 review.** Claude Opus 5 (CVP), three streams (sealing, request authorization, machine state), read-only on the reference machine. 17 findings: 4 high, 7 medium, 6 low. The locality gate, journal scores, camera binding, sticky sealing, the recovery path and the presence binding were fixed; the credential scope moved to root-only and the pre-migration snapshot was deleted.
- **2026-09-22, adversarial review.** Claude Opus 5 (CVP), five streams, read-only. 2 critical, 4 high, 9 medium, 9 low. Twelve fixed in the first pass, the critical consent-chain finding fixed in a narrowed form in the second; the unmeasured false-accept rate accepted and stated above.
- **2026-09-23, round 3.** Seven Claude Opus 5 (CVP) streams by area, one independent Codex (gpt-6-astra) pass over the whole system (12 findings), and fresh-context verifiers. 50 work items: 1 blocker, 9 high, 12 medium, 24 low, 4 info; 4 refuted. No memory-safety bug found. The trusted-path marker was declined with the residual recorded above.
- **2026-09-24, round 4: security, simplicity and release.** Thirteen Claude Opus 5 (CVP) streams (IPC, consent, store, PAM and CLI, engine, Omarchy scripts and shell, packaging, docs, hygiene, simplification, the companion package, the Quickshell fix) and three verifiers. 197 findings merged to 128 work items: 1 critical, 7 high, 41 medium, 72 low, 7 info. A formed 3D mask was ruled out of scope.
- **2026-09-25 and 26, two cleanup waves.** The code changes for round 4 went through an adversarial review and four re-review passes (wave 1), then two re-reviews of the cleanup wave (wave 2), each by Claude Opus 5.5 on a fresh context; the security-path re-review compared the old and new request routing across all 262,144 field combinations and found no behavior change beyond the intended list. The socket access-list fix after a crashed enrollment went through two review rounds of its own.
- **2026-09-21 and 24, the Quickshell polkit fix.** A Codex review of the patch and its write-up (8 findings: 1 blocker, 7 should-fix, all addressed), then the round 4 stream confirmed the use-after-free closed under AddressSanitizer.
